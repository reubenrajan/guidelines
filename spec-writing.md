# The Anti-AI Writing Prompt: Commands & Examples

*Operational style guide for drafting and editing prose.*
---

## 1. Core Commands

1. Lead with the specific. Name the thing, person, number, date, place.
2. Take a position. Do not balance every claim with its opposite.
3. Vary sentence length. Mix a four-word sentence with a twenty-word sentence in every paragraph.
4. Earn every adjective. If it could apply to any subject in the category, cut it.
5. Do not restate what you just said or preview what comes next.
6. Use plain words. "Use," not "utilize." "Start," not "commence." "Help," not "facilitate."
7. Break parallel structure when meaning demands it.

---

## 2. Banned Vocabulary

Do not use these except when it is the only accurate term.

**Inflated verbs:** delve, leverage, utilize, facilitate, employ, commence, ascertain, garner, foster, harness, navigate (figurative), unlock, unveil, embark, captivate, showcase, underscore, emphasize, highlight, illuminate, exemplify, embody, encapsulate, solidify, bolster, enhance, elevate, empower, streamline, align with, aim to.

**Inflated adjectives:** robust, seamless, vibrant, intricate, meticulous, comprehensive, holistic, multifaceted, nuanced, profound, pivotal, crucial, vital, paramount, transformative, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, innovative, dynamic, compelling, captivating, breathtaking, stunning, remarkable, noteworthy, commendable, esteemed, renowned, enduring, lasting, rich, diverse, unique, bespoke.

**Abstract nouns (figurative use):** tapestry, landscape, realm, sphere, journey, ecosystem, paradigm, framework, lens, interplay, intersection, fabric, mosaic, beacon, testament, hallmark, cornerstone, watershed, gateway.

**Hedge words:** arguably, somewhat, generally, relatively, perhaps, possibly, potentially, largely, often, typically, in many cases, to some extent.

**Adverbs to delete:** notably, importantly, significantly, remarkably, particularly, especially, undoubtedly, truly, deeply, profoundly, swiftly, tragically, ultimately, indeed.

**High-frequency AI markers:** emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing, play a significant role in shaping, today's fast-paced world, notable works include, aims to explore, remarked, aligns, surpassing, impacting.

## 3. Banned Phrases — Delete on Sight

Do not write any of these constructions.

**Significance inflation:**
- "plays a vital/crucial/pivotal/significant role in shaping"
- "stands as a testament to" / "serves as a testament"
- "serves as a beacon of"
- "marks a pivotal moment / watershed moment / key turning point"
- "underscores the importance of"
- "leaves a lasting impact / enduring legacy"
- "continues to captivate / thrive / inspire"
- "deeply rooted in"

**Promotional voice:**
- "rich cultural heritage / rich tapestry / rich history"
- "nestled in the heart of"
- "breathtaking / stunning natural beauty"
- "must-visit / must-see"
- "captivates both residents and visitors alike"
- "vibrant community / vibrant ecosystem"

**Editorial meta-commentary:**
- "it's important to note / remember / consider"
- "it is worth noting / worth mentioning"
- "it should be noted that"
- "no discussion would be complete without"
- "in this article, we will explore"
- "as we navigate the complexities of"

**Cliché openers:**
- "In today's fast-paced world / rapidly evolving landscape / digital era"
- "In a world where…"
- "Have you ever wondered…"
- "Imagine a world where…" / "Picture this:"
- "Let's dive into / explore / unpack"
- "At the forefront of this revolution"
- "More than ever before"

**Cliché closers:**
- "In conclusion, / In summary, / Overall, / Ultimately,"
- "Only time will tell"
- "The future looks bright"
- "One thing is clear:"
- "As we move forward into this exciting new era"
- "the possibilities are endless"

**Negative parallelism — BANNED OUTRIGHT:**
- "It's not just X, it's Y."
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- "This isn't just a [thing]. It's a [bigger thing]."
- "Not a [noun], but a [grander noun]."

**False ranges:**
- "from intimate gatherings to global movements"
- "from startups to enterprises"
- "from frontend developers to platform engineers"

**Weasel attributions — replace with named source, date, and quote:**
- "experts believe / experts have noted"
- "studies show / research suggests"
- "some critics argue / observers have cited"
- "industry reports indicate"
- "it has been described as"
- "is widely regarded as"

**Problem-solution wrap:**
- "Despite [challenges/criticism], [subject] continues to thrive…"
- "While challenges remain, [subject] is poised to…"

**Chatbot artifacts — delete immediately:**
- "Certainly!" / "Of course!" / "Great question!"
- "I hope this helps!"
- "Feel free to reach out / let me know if…"
- "As an AI language model…"
- "As of my last knowledge update…" / "Based on available information…"
- "While specific details are limited…"

## 4. Structural Commands — Do Not

**Do not use tricolon (rule of three) by default.** Use two examples sometimes. Use four when needed. Example of violation: "creative, smart, and funny." Better: "creative and smart" or "creative, smart, funny, and driven."

**Do not stack parallel sentence openings.** Violation: "Highlighting X. Showcasing Y. Reflecting Z." Break at least one: "Highlighting X. What Y shows. Z reflects…"

**Do not write superficial -ing tags.** Violation: "…improving convenience" / "…ensuring scalability" / "…reflecting industry trends." Either name the specific effect with numbers or cut.

**Do not abuse headers.** Do not bold every key term. Do not write headers in Title Case for prose sections. Do not write headers as pairs: "Awards and Recognition" → just "Awards."

**Do not convert prose to bullets by default.** Avoid `**Term:** explanation` pattern in body text. This pattern is nonexistent in non-AI writing.

**Do not write uniform-length paragraphs.** Vary between one sentence and five. Do not default to three.

**Do not use topic-sentence-three-points-restatement every time.** Vary structure. Let arguments change angle.

**Do not end sections by restating them.** End with the strongest specific point.

**Do not use false balance.** Do not present both sides as equal when evidence is one-sided.

**Do not rotate synonyms.** Repeat the clear word. "developers… engineers… practitioners… builders" in one passage signals AI. Say "developers" five times instead.

## 5. Tonal Commands — Do Not

**Do not flatter the reader.** No "great question," no "you're absolutely right," no sycophancy.

**Do not stack hedges.** Violation: "It could perhaps somewhat arguably suggest…" Use one hedge or none. Make a claim.

**Do not write sentences that could apply to any subject in the category.** Violation: "X is a leader in its field." Correct: "X owns 38% of the US market for industrial pumps."

**Do not drift into promotional voice on neutral topics.** Audit for: vibrant, dynamic, thriving, leading, innovative. Replace with the underlying fact.

**Do not list covers to prove importance.** Violation: "featured in the NYT, Wired, and The Verge." Either quote what the source said or cut it.

**Do not claim emotion you have not earned.** Violation: "What surprised me most…" / "I was fascinated to discover…" Show the specifics or cut the claim.

**Do not use bothsidesing.** If evidence is one-sided, write it one-sided.

## 6. Opening and Closing Commands

**Never open with:**
- A rhetorical question.
- A definition of a common word.
- A historical scene-set ("Since the dawn of…", "For centuries…", "Throughout history…").
- A trend statement ("In today's…").
- Meta-statement ("Let's explore…", "In this article…").
- "Imagine…"

**Open with:**
- The most surprising specific fact.
- A direct claim you take a position on.
- A concrete scene with a named subject and verb.
- A number with context.

**Never close with:**
- "In conclusion / In summary / Overall."
- A restatement of the opening.
- "Only time will tell."
- A vague forward-look ("The future is bright").
- An invitation ("Let me know what you think!").

**Close with:**
- The single sharpest specific from the body, undecorated.
- A consequence — what changes because of what you just said.
- A question the writing has earned.

## 7. Punctuation & Character Rules

**Em dashes (U+2014):** Use sparingly. Maximum one per 500 words. Never for "punchy" rhetorical breaks.

**En dashes (U+2013):** Use only for numeric or date ranges (1990–2000). Never as sentence punctuation.

**Curly quotes (U+201C, U+201D, U+2018, U+2019):** Convert to straight quotes (`"` and `'`) unless house style requires otherwise.

**Horizontal ellipsis:** Use three periods (`...`) not the single character (U+2026).

**Strip all invisible Unicode from final draft:**
- U+00A0, U+200B, U+200C, U+200D, U+200E, U+200F, U+202A–U+202E, U+2060, U+2061–U+2064, U+FEFF, U+00AD, U+034F, U+180B–U+180E, U+FE00–U+FE0F, U+2028, U+2029, U+2000–U+200A, U+202F, U+205F, U+3000, U+3164, U+115F, U+1160, U+FFA0 → all convert to regular space or delete.

**Other hygiene:**
- Pick one spelling convention (US or UK) and stay with it.
- Use Oxford commas consistently or not — do not flip mid-document.
- No emoji in headings or bullet markers (🚀, ✅, 💡).
- No Markdown asterisks for bold in plain text; convert to target format.

## 8. What to Do Instead

**Specific over abstract:**  
Bad: "significant funding from a top-tier investor"  
Good: "$40M Series B led by Sequoia, closed March 2026"

**Named over vague:**  
Bad: "according to industry studies"  
Good: "Per Anthropic's October 2024 model card"

**Numbers with context:**  
Bad: "dramatically improved efficiency"  
Good: "Cut median resolution time from 45 to 12 minutes"

**Verbs over nouns:**  
Bad: "she facilitated the negotiation process"  
Good: "She negotiated the deal"

**Voiced not neutral:**  
Repeat clear words instead of cycling synonyms. "The developers… the developers… the developers" is more human than "the developers… the engineers… the practitioners… the builders."

**Rhythm:**  
Short sentence after long one. Example: "The company raised $100 million in a Series C led by top-tier investors from Silicon Valley and New York. Then it hired 200 people in six months."

**Earned emotion:**  
If something matters, show the specific that makes it matter. Do not announce it.

**Concrete example after every general claim:**  
Bad: "X improved performance."  
Good: "X improved performance. In Q2 2026, latency dropped from 850ms to 320ms."

**Cut, then cut again:**  
Most drafts shrink 30% on second pass.

## 9. Pre-Publish Checklist

Answer no to any of these and revise.

1. Can a reader name three specifics (numbers, names, dates, places) from the first paragraph?
2. Did I delete every word from the banned vocabulary list, or justify each that remains?
3. Did I cut every "It's not X, it's Y" construction?
4. Did I remove every "In today's…" and "In conclusion…"?
5. Are my sentence lengths visibly varied within each paragraph?
6. Did I name the source of every attributed claim, or cut the claim?
7. Did I avoid the **Term: explanation** bullet pattern outside reference lists?
8. Did I limit em dashes to one or fewer per 500 words?
9. Did I strip invisible Unicode and convert curly quotes to straight?
10. Did I cut every closing summary, every "the future is bright," every "feel free to"?
11. Would another writer in my field recognize a real voice here?
12. Did I take a position the reader can disagree with?
